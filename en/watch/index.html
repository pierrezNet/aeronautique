UTC clock

This clock is set to UTC (Coordinated Universal Time). It uses your device's motion and light sensors for an immersive experience.

Tap the watch to allow access to the motion sensors and turn on the artificial horizon.

Why UTC?

In aviation, everyone sets their watch to the same time: Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), also known as “Zulu” time. Flight plans, METARs, TAFs, NOTAMs, take-off and landing times: everything is given in UTC followed by the letter Z (for example 1430Z). A crew crossing several time zones and a controller on the ground thus share the same reference, with no room for confusion.

In the United Kingdom, local time is UTC in winter and UTC + 1 in summer; in mainland France, it is UTC + 1 in winter and UTC + 2 in summer. When it is 14:30 in Paris in July, it is therefore 12:30 UTC.

A dial inspired by the instrument panel

The watch background is an artificial horizon, the instrument that shows the pilot the aircraft's attitude. On a phone or tablet, tilt the device: sky and ground bank just like on a real instrument. On iPhone and iPad, first tap the watch to allow access to the motion sensors.

Between 21:00 and 06:00 UTC, the dial switches to night mode: dimmed brightness and green hands, like cockpit lighting at night. On compatible browsers, the page theme also adapts to the ambient light.
